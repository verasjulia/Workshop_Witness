              With the European invasion, as happened in other Indigenous territories, Guarani territories were brutally reconfigured through forced displacement and extermination.

              In São Paulo, after the founding of the Pateo do Collegio settlement in 1554, the Tupi-Guarani populations who inhabited the region were subjected to systematic policies of confinement and enslavement.

              A founding landmark of the city, the Pateo do Collegio radiated colonization outward onto Jaraguá, one of the first regions where Europeans mined gold in colonial Brazil. Jaraguá then became a target of exploitation by bandeirantes, and was thus continuously depopulated of its native inhabitants.
