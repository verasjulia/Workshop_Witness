                What can be observed in the trajectory of Jaraguá’s heritagization is the emphasis placed by São Paulo’s safeguarding bodies on recognizing only colonial heritage, seeking to reinforce a bandeirante narrative of the city’s origin and identity — a narrative that erases Indigenous presence. 

                This is the case not only for Jaraguá’s heritage but for the whole city of São Paulo — Indigenous Territory —, which, through monuments such as the Pateo do Colégio and the Monumento às Bandeiras, builds its identity under the sign of colonial conquest. 

                One of the most emblematic examples of this colonialist narrative, officialized by São Paulo’s heritage bodies, is found in the heritage-listing project for Jaraguá State Park, drawn up by CONDEPHAAT in 1978.

                The original project proposed the creation of a tourist complex at Jaraguá Park celebrating the memory of the colonial conquest of Guarani lands, including the construction of a giant monumental statue of the Jesuit José de Anchieta, founder of the Pateo do Collegio settlement, atop Pico do Jaraguá. 

                As with the Christ the Redeemer monument in Rio de Janeiro, the project included the installation of a tram line to carry tourists up to the great monumental statue.
