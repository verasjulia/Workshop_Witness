                The nhandereko – the spatial and cultural practices of dwelling and cultivating the land of Guarani communities – is under constant threat from the predatory urbanization of São Paulo, which continues to devour territories, forests, and rivers along its edges. 

                As the metropolis expands, pursuing real-estate capital profits, the Guarani territory is increasingly hemmed in by urban infrastructure, real-estate developments, and invasions. Even though demarcated, the Indigenous Territory remains under growing spatial and political pressure. 

                During the participatory mapping workshops, in addition to the spatial practices of care and reparation of the land, several points of threat to the integrity of the Jaraguá territory and forests were identified.
